Water Damage We See Most in Austin

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Austin property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. On Austin roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Corpus Christi Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Drying Works on an Austin Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Austin property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

Extraction is always step one, since pumping and vacuuming remove water much faster than any drying machine. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Austin homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.
