What Usually Goes Wrong in El Paso Homes

Most water damage calls we take in El Paso trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. The crew from Corpus Christi Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in El Paso began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

Preventing Water Damage in El Paso Homes

Most of the water damage we see in El Paso homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Begin at the hoses. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and El Paso homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.
